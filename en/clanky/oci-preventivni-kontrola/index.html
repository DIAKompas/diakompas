Eyes and diabetes: do not wait for blurred vision

Good eyesight does not guarantee a healthy retina. Screening can find changes before you notice a problem.

Published: 13 November 2026

Educational content only; it does not replace individual advice from a healthcare professional.

What can change

Persistently high glucose can damage the small blood vessels of the retina. Early diabetic retinopathy may cause no symptoms. Blurred vision alone does not identify its cause.

What the examination involves

An eye care professional examines the back of the eye, often after drops widen the pupils. These drops can temporarily blur vision and increase light sensitivity. Ask in advance about travelling home and whether you can drive.

Agree your screening schedule

When screening begins and how often it is repeated depend on diabetes type, previous findings and other circumstances. Pregnancy may require closer monitoring. Agree a specific appointment with your diabetes and eye care teams.

Do not wait for routine screening

Sudden vision loss, new flashes, many floating spots or a curtain-like shadow need urgent eye assessment. Do not wait for your next scheduled check.
