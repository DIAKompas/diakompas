Exercise and diabetes: a safe start

Physical activity can support fitness, insulin sensitivity, and mental well-being. The best plan is safe and manageable for you.

Published: 14 September 2026

Educational content only; it does not replace individual advice from a healthcare professional.

Start gradually

Short walks, cycling, or swimming can be good starting points. Increase duration and frequency gradually according to your health.

General guidance

The ADA recommends that most adults with diabetes aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week, spread across several days, plus resistance exercise two to three times weekly. Recommendations should be individualized.

Blood glucose and treatment

For people using insulin or certain other medicines, exercise can increase the risk of hypoglycemia (low blood glucose). Before exercising, know the monitoring, food, and treatment plan agreed with your care team.

When to stop

Stop activity and seek help if you experience chest pain, severe shortness of breath, dizziness, or unusual weakness. Discuss suitable activities with your clinician if you have diabetes complications.
