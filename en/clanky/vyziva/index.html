Eating with diabetes: what really helps

There is no single meal plan that works for everyone. Long-term sustainability and an individualized plan matter.

Published: 28 September 2026

Terms in this article (1)
  • Carbohydrates: more than sugars — Carbohydrates include sugars, starch and fibre. They occur in grains, fruit, milk, legumes and vegetables.
Educational content only; it does not replace individual advice from a healthcare professional.

Core principles

Choose a varied diet with plenty of vegetables, fiber, good-quality protein sources, and appropriate amounts of fat. Limit sugar-sweetened drinks and frequently consumed highly processed foods.

Carbohydrates are not forbidden

The amount and timing of carbohydrates can affect blood glucose. Pay attention to portions, meal composition, and your own response; people using insulin may benefit from learning carbohydrate counting with their diabetes care team.

What to try in practice

Build meals around vegetables, an appropriate portion of starchy food, and a protein source. A registered dietitian experienced in diabetes can help develop an individual meal plan.

Beware of one-size-fits-all promises

Extreme diets, fasting, and sudden dietary changes can be risky with some medicines. Discuss major changes with your clinician.
