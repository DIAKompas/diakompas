Teeth and gums: part of diabetes care

Bleeding gums deserve attention. Oral health belongs alongside regular foot and eye care.

Published: 13 November 2026

Educational content only; it does not replace individual advice from a healthcare professional.

Why diabetes matters for your mouth

Diabetes can increase the risk of gum disease and dry mouth. Inflammation and difficulty chewing can make everyday care and eating harder. Increased risk does not mean tooth loss is inevitable.

A practical routine

Brush twice a day with fluoride toothpaste. Clean between teeth regularly using a suitable aid; a dental hygienist can help you choose one. Discuss dry mouth and your medicines with a healthcare professional.

Tell your dentist

Mention your diabetes, medicines and any episodes of low glucose. Before a major procedure, agree a food and medication plan with your care team. Do not stop medicines on the basis of a general article.

When to seek care

Recurring bleeding, swelling, loose teeth or a lesion that does not heal need dental assessment. Facial swelling with fever, trouble swallowing or difficulty breathing needs urgent help.
