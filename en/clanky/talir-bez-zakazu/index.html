Build a balanced plate without a list of forbidden foods

The plate method is a simple guide to meal composition. It does not set the same portion for everyone or replace carbohydrate counting when you need it.

Published: 13 November 2026

Terms in this article (1)
  • Carbohydrates: more than sugars — Carbohydrates include sugars, starch and fibre. They occur in grains, fruit, milk, legumes and vegetables.
Educational content only; it does not replace individual advice from a healthcare professional.

Three parts of a meal

Roughly half a standard plate can be non-starchy vegetables, one quarter protein and one quarter carbohydrate foods such as potatoes, whole grains or pulses. Pulses provide both carbohydrate and protein.

A home-cooking example

Add fish, tofu or chicken and a measured side to a vegetable salad. You do not need a separate diabetic meal. Consider drinks, sauces and extra bread too; they can add carbohydrate and energy.

Adjust portions to your needs

Plate size, hunger, activity and treatment influence suitable quantities. If you count carbohydrate for insulin, a plate picture alone is not enough. Use actual weights and labels as agreed in your plan.

One step for your next meal

Choose a favourite meal and add vegetables. If you have kidney complications, are pregnant or have other dietary requirements, coordinate changes with a dietitian.
