Haemoglobin: why HbA1c can sometimes be inaccurate

How red blood cells and anaemia can affect HbA1c.

Published: 14 October 2026

Terms in this article (2)
  • HbA1c: glycated haemoglobin — HbA1c forms when glucose binds to haemoglobin in red blood cells and roughly reflects glucose exposure over the previous 2–3 months.
  • CGM – continuous glucose monitoring — CGM uses a sensor to repeatedly estimate glucose in interstitial fluid, rather than directly in blood.
Educational content only; it does not replace individual advice from a healthcare professional.

What is haemoglobin?

Haemoglobin is a protein in red blood cells (erythrocytes) that carries oxygen from the lungs to tissues. It is the basis of HbA1c testing.

How does glycated haemoglobin form?

Glucose gradually binds to haemoglobin through glycation. HbA1c roughly reflects the previous 2–3 months, not current glucose.

Why does red cell lifespan matter?

Red cells usually live about 120 days. Faster breakdown can cause falsely low HbA1c, while other conditions can increase it.

When may HbA1c mislead?

Haemolytic anaemia may lower HbA1c and iron deficiency may raise it. Transfusions, haemoglobinopathies and advanced kidney disease may reduce reliability.

What if HbA1c disagrees with CGM?

Review measurement quality, the time period and factors affecting red cells. A difference does not automatically mean the sensor or laboratory is wrong.

Practical example

CGM repeatedly shows higher glucose while HbA1c is unexpectedly low. Shortened red cell lifespan is one possible explanation, not a diagnosis.

Key message

HbA1c is useful but imperfect. Discuss discrepancies with a clinician and do not change treatment without advice.
