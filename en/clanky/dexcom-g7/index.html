Dexcom G7: monitoring glucose and getting started with a sensor

The sensor shows both your current glucose value and its trend. How does Dexcom G7 differ from a blood glucose meter, what should you prepare before insertion, and where can you find the manufacturer's instruction video?

Published: 4 November 2026

Terms in this article (1)
  • CGM – continuous glucose monitoring — CGM uses a sensor to repeatedly estimate glucose in interstitial fluid, rather than directly in blood.
Educational content only; it does not replace individual advice from a healthcare professional.
This article describes the ten-day Dexcom G7 using Czech-market documentation. Always follow the instructions supplied with your specific sensor. Similar product names and videos from other countries do not guarantee identical conditions of use.

What the sensor actually measures

CGM means continuous glucose monitoring. Glucose is a sugar the body uses for energy. A thin filament under the skin measures its concentration in interstitial fluid — the fluid around cells. A blood glucose meter instead measures a drop of blood. During rapid changes, the results may therefore differ. G7 sends a new reading approximately every five minutes.[1]

What you see on the screen

A compatible phone or receiver displays your glucose value, a graph and a trend arrow, showing whether glucose is rising, falling or changing more slowly. The system offers high and low glucose alerts. Read each value in the context of its trend and your symptoms; this article does not set insulin doses or personal targets.[1], [2]

Key facts for the Czech-market version

  • The sensor and transmitter form a single component.[2]
  • Warm-up takes approximately 30 minutes. During warm-up, do not rely on readings or alerts from the new sensor.[2], [1]
  • Wear time is up to 10 days, followed by an additional 12-hour replacement grace period. This does not mean the sensor can be extended indefinitely.[3]
  • Routine use does not require regular calibration — comparing and entering meter readings to adjust measurement.[2]

Before the first insertion

Check the exact phone model and operating system version against the Dexcom compatibility list. Having Android or iOS alone is not enough. If you do not want to use a phone, learn about the G7 receiver option. Configure the phone and alerts according to the instructions; Bluetooth being on does not guarantee you will hear every alert.[4], [1]

Instruction video: inserting the sensor

▶ Watch the official Dexcom video: How to Insert the G7 Sensor · 1:42

Direct link to the video

The official English manufacturer video is intended for the US market. The link opens a Dexcom page with a YouTube player. A Czech version and the availability of Czech subtitles have not been verified.

The video shows the automatic applicator. For insertion sites and other conditions, the Czech instructions for your product take precedence. Czech-market documentation lists the back of the upper arm and abdomen, and the upper buttocks for children aged 2 to 6. Do not confuse these conditions with US materials.[5], [6]

The basic sequence includes preparing the app or receiver, inserting the sensor, securing it with the overpatch, pairing with the code from the applicator, and warm-up. Follow the instructions and video for the detailed insertion movements; this overview does not replace them. Keep the applicator code to complete pairing.[7]

When a blood glucose meter is needed

If a reading or alert does not match your symptoms or expectations, use a blood glucose meter for treatment decisions. The same applies during warm-up or when a glucose value or trend arrow is missing. Do not ignore symptoms, and follow your agreed treatment plan.[1], [6]

Checking safety notices

The manufacturer has issued a notice about specific stolen G7 lots: 1725204004 and 1725069002. These sensors must not be used. The notice does not apply to every G7 sensor; if the lot matches, follow the manufacturer's instructions and contact support.[8]

What to remember

G7 adds a view of glucose trends to individual measurements. It is useful when you understand its display, configure your device correctly and know when to use a blood glucose meter. For comparison, continue with our article on reading meter and CGM values.
