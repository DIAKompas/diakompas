Diabetic foot care: everyday prevention

Regular foot checks and prompt attention to changes can help prevent serious complications.

Published: 21 September 2026

Educational content only; it does not replace individual advice from a healthcare professional.

Daily checks

Examine your feet, including the soles and spaces between the toes. Look for pressure marks, blisters, cracks, changes in color, and new injuries. Use a mirror or ask someone you trust if you cannot see your feet.

Footwear and hygiene

Wear well-fitting shoes and socks that do not cause pressure. Check inside your shoes before putting them on. Wash and dry your feet gently, especially between the toes; avoid walking barefoot unless your clinician advises otherwise.

Professional foot checks

How often your feet need checking depends on individual risk, sensation, and circulation. Reduced sensation or a previous foot ulcer may require more frequent follow-up.

When to contact a clinician

A new wound, redness, swelling, discharge, darkening tissue, or rapid deterioration needs prompt professional assessment. Do not wait for pain, because sensation may be reduced.
