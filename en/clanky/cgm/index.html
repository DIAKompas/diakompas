Blood glucose meters and CGM: understanding your readings

Finger-stick glucose meters and continuous glucose monitoring provide different but complementary information.

Published: 7 September 2026

Terms in this article (1)
  • CGM – continuous glucose monitoring — CGM uses a sensor to repeatedly estimate glucose in interstitial fluid, rather than directly in blood.
Educational content only; it does not replace individual advice from a healthcare professional.

What does a glucose meter measure?

A standard meter measures glucose in a drop of capillary blood. The device, test strips, and correct technique all affect the reliability of the result.

What does CGM show?

A sensor continuously estimates glucose in interstitial fluid and displays trends over time; some systems also provide alerts. During rapid changes, sensor readings may differ from blood glucose values.

When a reading does not match how you feel

If symptoms do not match a CGM reading, follow the manufacturer’s instructions and your treatment plan. A finger-stick meter check may be needed.

Choosing a device

The choice depends on treatment, individual needs, availability, and training. Select a suitable system and interpret its data together with your diabetes care team.
