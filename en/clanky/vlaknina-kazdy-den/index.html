Fibre: adding it to everyday meals

Oats, pulses, vegetables and whole fruit provide fibre. You do not need to change your entire diet at once.

Published: 13 November 2026

Terms in this article (2)
  • Carbohydrates: more than sugars — Carbohydrates include sugars, starch and fibre. They occur in grains, fruit, milk, legumes and vegetables.
  • TIR – time in range — TIR is the percentage of time sensor glucose stays within a defined target range.
Educational content only; it does not replace individual advice from a healthcare professional.

What fibre does

Fibre is a plant-food component that the body does not digest like starch and sugars. It supports digestion, and some types slow nutrient absorption. A high-fibre meal can still contain substantial carbohydrate.

Try one simple swap

Replace some refined grain with pulses or a wholegrain option. Add oats and whole fruit to plain yoghurt. Portion size still matters; carbohydrate depends on the food and weight used.

Increase gradually

A sudden large increase can cause bloating. Start with a small portion and notice tolerance. Drink adequately, but discuss changes if you have a prescribed fluid restriction or a special diet.

Check which label you use

On EU food labels, fibre is listed separately from carbohydrate. Do not automatically subtract it again from that carbohydrate figure. Labels in other countries may use different conventions. Follow the counting method agreed with your educator.
