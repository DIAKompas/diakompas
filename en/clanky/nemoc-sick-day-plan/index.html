Illness and diabetes: make a sick-day plan

Infection can raise glucose even when you eat less. Agree a safe plan while you are well.

Published: 13 November 2026

Educational content only; it does not replace individual advice from a healthcare professional.

What the plan should cover

Write down with your team how often to check glucose, when to check ketones and whom to contact. Include fluids, food and instructions for your specific medicines. Some medicines need special sick-day rules; one instruction does not fit everyone.

Do not improvise insulin changes

If you need insulin, do not omit it on your own just because you are not eating. Doses and adjustments belong in your individual plan. This article does not set doses. Contact your diabetes team if you do not have a plan.

Fluids and records

Check that you can drink and keep fluids down. Record readings, symptoms and medicines taken to help your team assess the situation. Follow any clinician-prescribed fluid restriction for another condition.

When to get urgent help

Repeated vomiting, inability to keep fluids down, abdominal pain, deep rapid breathing, confusion or elevated ketones need prompt medical contact. For loss of consciousness or severe breathing problems, call 112 in the EU, or your local emergency number elsewhere. Give nothing by mouth to an unconscious person.
