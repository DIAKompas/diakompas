When diabetes feels exhausting: understanding diabetes distress

Monitoring, planning and constant decisions can be tiring. Asking for support is part of care, not a weakness.

Published: 13 November 2026

Terms in this article (1)
  • TIR – time in range — TIR is the percentage of time sensor glucose stays within a defined target range.
Educational content only; it does not replace individual advice from a healthcare professional.

Name the burden

Diabetes distress is the emotional burden of living with diabetes. It can involve worry about results, fatigue with routines or feeling overwhelmed. It is not automatically the same as depression, although both deserve attention.

Start with one obstacle

Identify what is hardest now: alarms, food planning, costs or other people's reactions. One specific problem is easier to work on with your team than the feeling that you must fix everything.

Support in practice

Ask someone close to you for a specific kind of help, or review one part of your routine with a diabetes educator. Agree a realistic next step. Glucose readings are information for decisions, not a judgement of your character.

When to seek professional support

Persistent sadness, anxiety, loss of interest or difficulties that disrupt daily life and self-care deserve discussion with a doctor or psychologist. If you do not feel safe, seek immediate help and do not stay alone.
