Type 1 and type 2 diabetes: what is the difference?

Both types of diabetes involve elevated blood glucose, but they develop differently and require individualized care.

Published: 14 October 2026

Educational content only; it does not replace individual advice from a healthcare professional.

What is type 1 diabetes?

It is an autoimmune condition in which the immune system destroys insulin-producing cells in the pancreas. People with type 1 diabetes need insulin treatment. The condition can begin in childhood or adulthood.

What is type 2 diabetes?

The body responds less effectively to insulin and, over time, may not produce enough insulin to meet its needs. Risk involves several factors, including genetics, age, and living conditions; it cannot be reduced to personal failure.

What symptoms may occur?

Frequent urination, increased thirst, fatigue, unexplained weight loss, or blurred vision may warrant medical assessment. A healthcare professional confirms the diagnosis with laboratory tests.

When should you seek urgent help?

Seek urgent medical care for vomiting, abdominal pain, rapid deep breathing, confusion, or severe dehydration. These may indicate an acute complication.
