Foot warning signs: when not to wait

Pain may not reflect how serious a problem is. Know the difference between a planned review, urgent assessment and emergency help.

Published: 13 November 2026

Educational content only; it does not replace individual advice from a healthcare professional.

A new change: make contact today

A new blister, crack, open wound, discharge, smell, unexplained pain or change in foot colour or temperature warrants contact with your clinician or diabetes foot team today. Do not wait for a routine appointment or for pain to develop. If your usual team is unavailable, use an urgent care service.

A red, hot, swollen foot

This can signal infection or acute Charcot neuroarthropathy, which damages bones and joints. It may be almost painless and have no wound. Reduce weight-bearing and arrange urgent assessment today; a photograph or brief description cannot establish the cause.

Signs needing immediate emergency assessment

A wound with fever, rapidly spreading redness, black tissue or a suddenly cold pale foot needs immediate emergency care. Call your local emergency number (112 in the EU) for confusion, collapse, breathlessness or rapid general deterioration. Serious infection can occur without fever.

While arranging help

Reduce pressure on the area, protect a wound with a clean non-adherent dressing and avoid soaking it. Do not puncture blisters, cut skin or start leftover antibiotics. Have your medicine list and the time symptoms started ready; home care must not delay professional assessment.

What belongs in a planned review

Discuss longstanding tingling, reduced sensation or recurrent pressure marks without an acute change at a prompt booked appointment. Continue daily checks. If a wound, swelling, colour or temperature change develops, or the condition worsens, follow the faster action described above.
