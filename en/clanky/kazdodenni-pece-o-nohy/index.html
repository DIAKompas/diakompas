Daily foot care with diabetes: a simple routine

A few minutes each day can help you spot changes early. Checking matters even when nothing hurts.

Published: 13 November 2026

Educational content only; it does not replace individual advice from a healthcare professional.

Look at the whole foot

Check both feet once a day in good light, including the soles, heels and spaces between the toes. Look for blisters, cracks, pressure marks, redness, swelling or discharge. Use a mirror or ask someone to help. Contact a clinician today for a new wound rather than waiting for your next routine appointment.

Wash, dry and moisturise

Wash gently with lukewarm rather than hot water, without prolonged soaking. Dry carefully, including between the toes. Moisturise dry intact skin but avoid the spaces between toes and open wounds. Reduced sensation makes hot water and direct heating a burn risk.

Choose and check footwear

Wear suitable footwear and clean socks, even indoors, rather than going barefoot. Check inside shoes for stones and rough seams before putting them on. Introduce new shoes gradually and check your skin afterwards. If footwear causes pressure, have the fit addressed instead of repeatedly covering the same pressure mark.

Nails and hard skin

Cut nails straight across and gently file sharp edges. If you cannot see or reach your feet well, or have a high-risk foot problem, ask for professional care. Do not cut away corns or calluses yourself or use chemical corn-removal products.

Make professional checks a habit

Ask for a comprehensive foot assessment at least yearly, with more frequent reviews if your risk calls for them. Know whom to contact for new symptoms. If you already have a wound, follow your individual dressing and offloading plan; this daily routine is mainly for intact skin.
