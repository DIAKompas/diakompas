Kidney checks in diabetes: understanding eGFR and urine albumin

Kidney damage can develop without pain. Blood and urine tests therefore matter even when you feel well.

Published: 13 November 2026

Educational content only; it does not replace individual advice from a healthcare professional.

Two complementary tests

eGFR estimates how well the kidneys filter blood, usually using blood creatinine. The urine albumin-to-creatinine ratio, or UACR, checks for protein leaking into urine. These tests look at different aspects of kidney health; one normal result does not rule out a change in the other.

A result needs context

Infection or dehydration can affect results. Your clinician considers the trend and may repeat a test. A laboratory number alone is not an instruction to change treatment.

What you can do

Ask about both tests and when they should be repeated. Follow your agreed glucose and blood pressure plan. Supplements and non-prescription medicines are not automatically safe for kidneys; discuss their use with a clinician or pharmacist.

Your next appointment

Write down two questions: When were my kidneys last checked? How are my results changing? Seek medical advice for markedly reduced urination, swelling or sudden deterioration.
