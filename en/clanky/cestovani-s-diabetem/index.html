Travelling with diabetes: prepare before you leave

A different routine, unfamiliar food and delays can affect glucose. A little preparation makes travel easier.

Published: 13 November 2026

Terms in this article (2)
  • Carbohydrates: more than sugars — Carbohydrates include sugars, starch and fibre. They occur in grains, fruit, milk, legumes and vegetables.
  • CGM – continuous glucose monitoring — CGM uses a sensor to repeatedly estimate glucose in interstitial fluid, rather than directly in blood.
Educational content only; it does not replace individual advice from a healthcare professional.

Keep essentials accessible

Carry medicines, monitoring equipment, spare supplies and fast-acting carbohydrate where you can reach them. Allow for delays and keep a medicine list. For flights, check airline and airport rules because local requirements vary.

Protect supplies from temperature extremes

Do not leave medicines or test strips in a hot car. Protect insulin from freezing and direct contact with ice. Follow the specific product leaflet for storage temperatures and time limits after opening.

Time zones and security screening

Discuss changes to insulin timing with your diabetes team before travel. Check the pump or CGM manufacturer's instructions for X-rays and scanners; devices do not all follow the same rules. Request an appropriate inspection method.

Have a backup plan

Save your team's contact details, local care options and insurance information. Carry water and suitable food for delays. Do not drive when unwell, and do not delay necessary medical help.
